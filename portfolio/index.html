Health Here is a mobile web app that finds doctors, hospitals and clinics around you, checks whether they accept your plan, and keeps emergency help one tap away.

Open the app See how I built it

Finding the right care shouldn't take a dozen phone calls.

Map apps show what's nearby, but not whether a place takes your insurance, whether it's open, or which one is actually good. And when something is really wrong, nobody wants to dig through menus. I designed Health Here around three people.

A parent with a referral

Her child was referred to a specialist. She needs one nearby, in her insurance network, that she can afford.

Someone comparing hospitals

He wants a highly rated hospital that takes his plan, with no surprise costs and no in-app fees.

An older adult in an emergency

She needs the nearest emergency room and a way to call 911, in large text, without signing in first.

From a flowchart to a working app

Before writing any code, I mapped every path through the app: opening it as a guest or with an account, onboarding, the two locators, and the emergency button that has to work from anywhere.

  1. Map the journeys

    Wrote user stories for each person and drew the flow from opening the app to getting care.

  2. Design for real hands

    Started on a phone: big tap targets, readable text, and the emergency button always within reach of a thumb.

  3. Connect real data

    Hooked up live places from Google, accounts from Supabase, and a reader for clinics' own insurance pages.

  4. Test and fix

    Tried it on phones and laptops in different browsers, then fixed what broke: layouts, location accuracy, email links.

What it does

Hover over a phone (or tap it) to watch a short recording of the real app, made with example places.

Search for what you actually need

Pick hospitals and clinics, or a doctor by specialty, from cardiology to pediatrics. Results appear as a list and on a map, with today's hours, ratings, and one tap to call or get directions.

  • "Best match" puts the most useful places first: ones that take your plan, with trusted ratings, open now, not too far.
  • A perfect 5.0 from two reviews doesn't outrank a 4.6 from nine hundred.

Know if they take your insurance

There's no free public list of which doctor takes which plan, so Health Here reads each clinic's own insurance page and looks for your plan among about 60 insurers, from Aetna to Medicaid.

  • Every result says what it found, with a link to the page it came from.
  • If the page couldn't be read, it says "call to confirm" instead of guessing.

Readable for everyone

Settings include larger text and high contrast, and the whole app works in English, Spanish and Korean. Every button is big enough to tap without precision.

  • Try it with no account: guest mode uses every feature and saves no profile.
  • Location can be turned off; then it searches near an address you type.
  • Accounts can be deleted for good, right from Settings.

How it works

Your phone talks to the Health Here server, which does the lookups. API keys never reach the browser.

Built with Next.js, React, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS. Accounts in Supabase, places from the Google Places API, maps with Leaflet and OpenStreetMap, hosted on Vercel.

Respectful by default

  • Guest mode keeps almost nothingNo profile is saved; only the search location stays on your own device, for 7 days.
  • Location is your choicePrecise GPS when you want it, a typed address when you don't, and it shows how accurate it is.
  • Your data is yoursEach account can only read its own settings, and deleting it removes everything.
  • Honest about uncertaintyWhen the app can't confirm your insurance, it tells you to call instead of guessing.

What was hard, and what I did about it

The real world is messier than a design file. Three problems taught me the most.

No insurance database

There's no free source that says which doctor takes which plan, and I didn't want the app to guess.

Read the clinic's own website

The server reads each clinic's insurance and billing pages, finds plan names, skips sentences like "we don't accept", and links to the source page.

Laptops guess your location

Without GPS, browsers can be off by miles, so "nearby" results weren't actually nearby.

Show the accuracy, offer an address

The app waits for the most precise reading, shows it ("accurate to about 40 ft"), and warns when it's rough, with address search as a backup.

Email links that broke

Password-reset links only worked in the same browser that asked for them, so opening the email on a phone failed.

Links that work anywhere

I changed how links sign people in so they work on any device, and expired links now explain what happened and offer a new one.

What's next

Health Here works today, and there's a clear list of what would make it better.

  • Real individual doctors from the U.S. government's NPI Registry, not just practices.
  • Verified insurance data from insurer directories, alongside the website reader.
  • Testing with older adults, then simplifying whatever slows them down.
  • More languages, starting with Chinese and Vietnamese.
